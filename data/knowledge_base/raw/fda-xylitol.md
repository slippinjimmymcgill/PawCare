## About xylitol

Xylitol is a sweetener found in a wide range of products. It might also go by other names, including wood sugar, birch sugar, and birch bark extract.

Products that may contain xylitol include:

- Sugar-free gum
- Sugar-free candy and mints
- Sugar-free desserts and baked goods
- Sugar-free jams, jellies, and nut butters
- Mouthwashes, oral rinses, and toothpastes
- Cough syrups and liquid medicines for adults and children
- Other types of medicines like nasal sprays
- Dietary supplements (like fiber gummies and sleep aids)

## Signs of xylitol poisoning

Xylitol poisoning can start within 20 minutes of eating a product containing it. Signs include:

- Vomiting
- Decreased activity level
- Weakness and collapse
- Difficulty walking or standing
- Shaking or seizures
- Coma
- Liver failure
- Bleeding problems

Xylitol poisoning can kill your dog. If you think your dog has eaten a product containing xylitol, call your vet, an emergency animal clinic, or an animal poison control center right away. Every second counts.

## Why xylitol is dangerous to dogs but not people

In both people and dogs, the level of blood sugar is controlled by the release of insulin from the pancreas. In people, xylitol does not stimulate the release of insulin from the pancreas. It is different in dogs: when a dog eats something containing xylitol, the xylitol is more quickly absorbed into the bloodstream and can cause a potent release of insulin from the pancreas. This rapid release of insulin can cause a rapid and profound decrease in the level of blood sugar (hypoglycemia), which can occur within 10 to 60 minutes of eating the xylitol. Untreated, this hypoglycemia can quickly be life-threatening.

Symptoms of xylitol poisoning in dogs include vomiting, followed by decreased activity, weakness, staggering, incoordination, collapse, and seizures. Because hypoglycemia and other serious effects may not occur for up to 12 to 24 hours, a dog may need to be hospitalized for medical monitoring.

Xylitol does not appear to be as dangerous for cats; cats appear to be spared, at least in part, because they generally do not eat sweet foods. Ferret owners should be careful, as ferrets have been known to develop low blood sugar and seizures after eating products containing xylitol.
